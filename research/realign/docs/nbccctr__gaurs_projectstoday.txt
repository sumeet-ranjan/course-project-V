Gaurs Group, a realty developer, will make an investment of Rs 2,124 crore in three stuck Amrapali projects it had taken over from the state-run NBCC.

Wednesday, March 24, 2021 Residential Projects Today News

![Image 1: Gaurs Group to infuse Rs 2,124 cr in three Amrapali projects](https://api.projectstoday.com/storage/newsImages/Residential/building-city-New.jpg)Illustrative image – not actual.

Gaurs Group, a realty developer, will make an investment of Rs 2,124 crore in three stuck Amrapali projects it had taken over from the state-run NBCC.

The Group has been awarded contracts for Amrapali Centurian Park, Verona Heights and Heartbeat City with the total inventory of 10,964 flats. The company is meeting the construction cost through internal accruals and bank guarantees.

The company is looking to increase the construction portfolio to Rs 7,000 crore in five years.

Gaurs Group has delivered over 50 commercial and residential projects spreading over 55 million sq ft and is currently not executing any large-scale project. The Group has also decided to diversify towards infrastructure.

The company has no large-scale real estate projects as of now and it has sold the majority of its under-construction inventory. In the Amrapali projects, the company is only executing it and responsibility of sales lies with NBCC.

The Group’s wholly-owned subsidiary, Gaursons Hitech Infrastructure, is executing these projects.

NBCC is involved in the completion of 23 housing projects of Amrapali. The estimated cost of completing these projects is around Rs 8,500 crore. The projects will be completed within 36 months of the allotment of the tender.

## Electrolyser manufacturers seek approved list from Centre

Electrolyser manufacturers in India have urged the Central government to introduce an approved list of domestic suppliers, similar to the Approved List of Models and Manufacturers (ALMM) for solar modules.

Monday, November 23, 2026 Hydrogen/Other Gases Projects Today News

![Image 3: Electrolyser manufacturers seek approved list from Centre](https://api.projectstoday.com/storage/newsImages/HydrogenOtherGases/Hydrogen%20&%20Other%20Gases-2-New.jpg)Illustrative image – not actual.

Electrolyser manufacturers in India have urged the Central government to introduce an approved list of domestic suppliers, similar to the Approved List of Models and Manufacturers (ALMM) for solar modules.

The proposed mechanism would encourage green hydrogen project developers and public sector companies to procure equipment from Indian manufacturers, reducing dependence on imported electrolysers. More than 20 companies, including Reliance Industries, L&T Electrolysers, Adani New Industries, Greenko Hydrogen and John Cockerill, have secured opportunities to establish domestic electrolyser manufacturing facilities. However, delays in financial closure and equipment procurement for green ammonia projects have created uncertainty over the sector's order pipeline.

The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy has allowed first-tranche manufacturers flexibility to commission facilities by August 2027 and defer their PLI incentive periods. Industry participants believe stronger domestic procurement requirements are necessary to support capacity utilisation, manufacturing investments and long-term sector growth.
