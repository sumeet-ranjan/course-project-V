NBCC (India) has awarded a contract to Varindera Constructions, Gurugram for the redevelopment of GPRA Colony in Sarojini Nagar, New Delhi. The order is worth Rs 955.82 crore.

The work entails redevelopment Type-V quarters (1048 nos.), six community halls and allied works on engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) contracts basis (Package IV-B).

The contract is expected to be completed in a 24-month timeframe, with operation and maintenance (O&M) to be carried out for five years.

involving an estimated investment of Rs 965 crore, including land costs. The hospitality development pipeline comprises approximately 650 keys across pilgrimage, leisure, heritage and urban destinations, with partnerships involving Indian Hotels Company (IHCL) and Minor Hotels. The company's real estate portfolio has an estimated topline potential exceeding Rs 4,400 crore across residential, mixed-use and urban redevelopment projects in five states. A major development is JK Vera, a 50-acre mixed-use township in Kanpur. Its first 6.5-acre phase will include a 150-key Taj Hotel, 14 branded residences, Grade-A office space and high-street retail.

Other developments include the completed Emerald Garden township, and the 164-residence Advait Senior Living project in Gurugram. Its hospitality pipeline includes projects in Bodh Gaya, Mussoorie, and other cities, alongside potential developments in Rishikesh, Jaipur and Kota.

## Electrolyser manufacturers seek approved list from Centre

Electrolyser manufacturers in India have urged the Central government to introduce an approved list of domestic suppliers, similar to the Approved List of Models and Manufacturers (ALMM) for solar modules.

Monday, November 23, 2026 Hydrogen/Other Gases Projects Today News

![Image 3: Electrolyser manufacturers seek approved list from Centre](https://api.projectstoday.com/storage/newsImages/HydrogenOtherGases/Hydrogen%20&%20Other%20Gases-2-New.jpg)Illustrative image – not actual.

Electrolyser manufacturers in India have urged the Central government to introduce an approved list of domestic suppliers, similar to the Approved List of Models and Manufacturers (ALMM) for solar modules.

The proposed mechanism would encourage green hydrogen project developers and public sector companies to procure equipment from Indian manufacturers, reducing dependence on imported electrolysers. More than 20 companies, including Reliance Industries, L&T Electrolysers, Adani New Industries, Greenko Hydrogen and John Cockerill, have secured opportunities to establish domestic electrolyser manufacturing facilities. However, delays in financial closure and equipment procurement for green ammonia projects have created uncertainty over the sector's order pipeline.

The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy has allowed first-tranche manufacturers flexibility to commission facilities by August 2027 and defer their PLI incentive periods. Industry participants believe stronger domestic procurement requirements are necessary to support capacity utilisation, manufacturing investments and long-term sector growth.
