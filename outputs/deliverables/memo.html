1. Question and method

When a localized, documented extreme event damages firms, does the damage travel along supply chains to firms outside the zone? I traced two events firm by firm. At each node the hit is confirmed from the firm’s own documents, links are verified with a dependence weight, and the node is measured by its CAR and its next quarterly numbers. The CAR uses a market model against NIFTY 500, with p-values from the same statistic on 1,000 random days; long windows also carry a zero-alpha CAR (beta kept). The disclosure that names the channel is found and confounds are ruled out. The contractor weights are brackets built from order books and contract run-rates, not reported sales shares. Each pathway rests on fewer than ten firms, so the statistics have little power and are read as such.

2. NCR, winter 2024: transmission inside the zone

The shock. CAQM’s GRAP orders stopped construction and demolition in Delhi-NCR: Stage III from 15 Nov 2024, Stage IV from 18 Nov. Restrictions were re-imposed on 16 Dec and returned for four Stage III spells in January–February 2025.

Hit builders.

  • NBCC (94% of its plant cost in NCR): “our major projects are Amrapali and 70% GPRA so these two projects this year severely hitted by the NGT”.
  • DLF (42%): “the 2 malls in the Delhi NCR were a little delayed because of the GRAP”.
  • Price: neither stock responded, +0.4 pp against developers with no NCR project over (−1,+5) (p = 0.91). DLF’s construction outflow rose in the ban quarter.
  • Signature Global (external), a Gurugram developer, does not mention the ban in its Q3 call.

Transmission ran builder to contractor at NCR sites.

  • Link: DLF is 3.4–10% of the sales of Ahluwalia (external).
  • Own exposure: Ahluwalia has 33.3% of its order book in NCR, so it is directly exposed. Its own sites were under the same ban.
  • Statements: “GRAP-IV is in place … going to impact this quarter” (18 Nov 2024). FY25 growth guided down to “8.5%-9%” (12 Feb 2025). “we lost approximately two to three months, two months in Q3 and nearly a month in Q4 of FY ’25” (2 Jun 2025).
  • Numbers: Q3 FY25 turnover −7.3% YoY; FY25 +6.3%, below the guidance.

Price evidence. The table gives Ahluwalia’s own abnormal return and the same net of the five unlinked contractors (all external). Two of them were takeover targets inside the windows: PSP (19 Nov) and Cemindia (25 Oct). The last column drops them. p-values are in brackets.

The other nodes.

  • B.L. Kashyap (DLF is 35–44% of its sales): its revenue was already falling before the ban, so it is not confirmed.
  • J. Kumar (external; NBCC is 1.6–3.4% of its sales): no effect, as expected at that dose.
  • NBCC’s GPRA contractors were unlisted in Nov 2024 (Varindera, Shapoorji Pallonji & Co. Pvt, Girdhari Lal Constructions Pvt), as was Gaursons on Amrapali (trade press only). There is no price or filed quarterly data, so the branch is closed.

Out of the zone: not detected.

  • No firm link. No document links any cement or steel firm to the builders. DLF’s FY24 construction outflow is at most 0.7–14% of any listed supplier’s revenue.
  • Cement makers that deliver to NCR (the test was specified before it was run):
    • Why this test: cement is sold near the plant, and Stage III shuts ready-mix plants and bans loading and unloading cement.
    • Dose: the share of each maker’s plants within 300 km of Delhi by road, from 205 plants in original documents: Mangalam 0.50, Shree 0.29, UltraTech 0.17, JK Cement 0.17, JK Lakshmi 0.14, Nuvoco 0.10, the Ambuja group 0.07. The maker’s other northern plants enter as a control. JK Cement, JK Lakshmi, Mangalam, Nuvoco and Ambuja are external.
    • Result: null. Coefficient −0.06 over (−1,+1) (p = 0.36); −0.01 over (−1,+5) (p = 0.90); +0.03 for 17 Dec (p = 0.70). Without takeover-related makers, without Mangalam, at 150 km or by capacity (IBM plant capacities), every p ≥ 0.23.
    • Volume: Q3 volume −0.24 (p = 0.41), matched in the quarter before the ban (−0.16).
    • Detectability: neither test could detect the largest loss the ban could cause: 16% of a quarter’s output for Mangalam (which publishes no volume), 9.6% for Shree.
    • Who delivers: the CCI order of 2017 names the same makers bidding district by district in Haryana. NBCC’s approved makes could not be read.
  • Steel: the approved rebar makes could not be read, so steel stops at the materiality bound (0.7–4.3% of a steel maker’s revenue).

3. Gujarat, August 2024: the one out-of-zone link

The shock.

  • The pack’s “Gujarat floods — Vadodara/central Gujarat” event was the deep depression over Saurashtra and Kutch that became Cyclonic Storm Asna (IMD, 30 Aug 2024).
  • The brief asks us to “refine each event’s window and footprint” (section 3). Here the footprint moved from Vadodara to the Saurashtra and Kutch coast.
  • The pack’s own chemical belt breaks at the first step. Its Vadodara sites went dark, but no firm says anything, and its stocks beat their controls (+5.5 pp, p = 0.05) while the controls themselves fell 4.7%.

Hit firms, all external to the pack:

  • Material: Tata Chemicals at Mithapur (“almost 10 days”; 40,000 t salt; about Rs 40 cr cost) and Archean in Kutch (Rs 40.18 cr of salt stock).
  • Confirmed but immaterial: Adani Ports (“4 days equivalent”; August cargo still +5%) and GE T&D (no damage).
  • Tata Chemicals’ share price shows nothing: abnormal return −0.1% over 26–30 Aug and +1.1% on its results day (daily s.d. 2.3%).
  • Archean’s 15.8% fall (11.7% net of specialty peers) spans the storm days. It fell 1–2.6% a day as Kachchh took 61–98 mm, then 4.8% on 2 Sep in 1 mm of rain. At about Rs 1,560 cr it is 30–40 times the booked loss. Not attributed.

The link: Tata Chemicals → Tata Consumer (edible salt). Both sides document it: Tata Consumer is the “single customer” for Tata Chemicals’ edible salt and sources “more than 85%” of its salt from it. Tata Chemicals is 18–24% of Tata Consumer’s material input cost.

What passed down the link.

  • Cost, stated by both firms. Mithapur “had to buy brine”; the cost reached Tata Consumer a quarter later. Tata Consumer raised Tata Salt once, “INR2 on a INR28 base, which is roughly around 7%”, from October 2024, “after a two-year hiatus”. Margin held.
  • A volume stall in the storm quarter, unexplained and consistent with lost supply. Tata Consumer’s salt volume growth went from +8% to flat. Tata Chemicals’ India salt volume fell 4.7% (“we were behind a bit from our plan”). It fell again in Q4, with no storm.
  • Magnitude: no document gives the share of the storm cost that was passed on. The increase is worth at least Rs 110 cr a year (7% of FY24 purchases of Rs 1,576.64 cr), about 2.8 times the storm’s whole cost to Tata Chemicals.

4. How strong is it

Claim Brief Evidence Strength
NCR: ban → builders → contractor, inside the zone H1 (builders); H3 (contractor, itself exposed) own statements at every node; contractor revenue, guidance, lost months; call days not distinguishable from zero documented; price evidence weak
NCR: out-of-zone suppliers H3 no firm link; cement delivery test null (p ≥ 0.23), also by capacity and without Mangalam; steel at the materiality bound not detected; the tests cannot detect the ban’s largest possible loss
Gujarat: Tata Chemicals → Tata Consumer → shelf price H2 both sides document the link and weights; cost, lag, one price increase, margin held; a storm-quarter volume stall at both firms documented; partly a catch-up; volume stall unexplained; no price effect at either firm
Gujarat: the pack’s chemical belt H1 dark sites, no statement, wrong-sign test breaks at the first step
Dose-response and placebos H4 eight contractors, no slope; placebo firms null null, with n = 8

Results tables.

  • (i) Direct test: no negative response at the confirmed NCR builders; a wrong-sign response in the Gujarat belt; the DiD is descriptive only. Zero-alpha CARs remove a drift artefact in long windows (Unitech’s alpha term is −17.7 of its −23.5 pp). No negative statistic becomes significant; two wrong-sign ones do (within-zone (+6,+20) +8.2%, p = 0.06; Vadodara (+21,+60) +20.7%, p = 0.03).
  • (ii) Dose-response: eight contractors, three linked and five at zero dose (dose = the builder’s share of the contractor’s sales × the builder’s NCR exposure). No relation with any CAR (exact permutation p = 0.67–1.00; without the two takeover targets, p = 0.47–0.93). The one significant slope, on Q3 sales, is matched on the quarter before the ban: it is B.L. Kashyap’s earlier decline. Gujarat has one link, so there is no slope.
  • (iii) Placebos: nulls. NCR’s 24 unexposed, unlinked firms: means −1.3% to +2.3%, p ≥ 0.30. Gujarat’s list drops Reliance Power, after SEBI’s order of 22 Aug 2024; without it, mean −3.6% (p = 0.21), median −2.1%.

Confounds carried:

  • NCR: Ahluwalia’s results on the ban’s day 0; the January recurrences; the small- and mid-cap sell-off of Jan–Feb 2025; Q4 seasonality; takeover targets in the comparison group.
  • Cement: northern prices; Shree’s stated choice of “price over volumes” (Q2 call, before the ban); deals in Oct–Dec 2024 (Ambuja–Orient, UltraTech–India Cements, Kesoram and Star); new capacity; Mangalam’s Q2 results inside the first windows.
  • Gujarat: tea costs; the price catch-up; soda-ash imports and new capacity; the fall in the Gujarat controls, all six firms (cause not identified).

5. What the manual trace caught that an automated pipeline would have missed or got wrong

  1. The pack’s own errors.
    • GRAP is dated 5 Nov 2024, but the orders came into force on 15 and 18 Nov.
    • Stock dates are one day early for 60 of 61 firms.
    • Substring matching puts 47 of 83 “Himachal” plant rows in other states (Guna, Junagadh, Bilaspur in Chhattisgarh).
    • Taken as given, all three put every window and exposure in the wrong place.
  2. The wrong firms. The pack’s Gujarat footprint points at a chemical belt that said nothing. The firms the storm hit are outside the pack and its districts, and the pack’s label for the event, a Vadodara flood, misses the storm.
  3. A link that does not exist. The scaffold’s cement/steel → construction link has no firm-level support. The real transmission is to contractors inside the zone.
    • Links counted earlier were wrong: Petronet without a confirmed source shock; the Aarti–UPL joint venture before it supplied; a Capacit’e–NBCC order dated after the event.
  4. Exposure mistaken for propagation. A third of Ahluwalia’s order book is in NCR, so its own sites were under the ban. A pipeline would credit its hit to the DLF link.
  5. Distance, not region. Cement travels a few hundred kilometres (reported lead distances of 285–381 km). A “northern capacity” share mixes the ban with the northern price cycle.
    • The plant data had to be built from annual reports, because the pack’s plant file holds project records.
    • Even then, a first pass missed Mangalam’s second plant, and Ambuja and ACC publish no plant capacities.
  6. Names. Companies call the GRAP order “the NGT ban”; one transcript spells GRAP “graph”; “ACC India Pvt Ltd”, a DLF contractor, is not ACC Ltd the cement maker. Keyword search and entity matching get all three wrong.
  7. Timing. Results come out after the close (Tata Consumer at 16:52; Ahluwalia on 14 Nov, before a holiday), calls run late (3.00 pm and 4.00 pm, priced the next day), and transcripts are filed days later. Dated by filing, the guidance cut would sit in the wrong window. My own first reading did exactly that.
  8. Unrelated moves and model artefacts. None of these is a shock response:
    • tea at Tata Consumer; the Tata Sons listing ruling and Ratan Tata’s death at Tata Chemicals;
    • SEBI’s order at Reliance Power; the Adani indictment at ACC and Ambuja;
    • takeover bids for two comparison contractors; estimation-window drift in long windows (RVNL −13.0 of −17.3 pp).
  9. Magnitudes. DLF’s construction spend rose; Archean’s price fall is 30–40 times its loss; the salt price increase is 2.8 times the storm’s cost, and “₹2” and “7%” are the same increase; the ban’s largest possible loss for a cement maker is below what the cross-maker tests can detect.
  10. Documents versus summaries. The 85% statement is on a web page, not in the report’s PDF; snippets got a results date and a mall wrong; some filings refuse automated access (NBCC’s tenders sit behind a CAPTCHA, not attempted).

6. Limitations

  • Each pathway rests on fewer than ten firms, and the cement tests cannot detect the ban’s largest possible loss.
  • NBCC’s approved makes, Nuvoco’s annual report and JK Cement’s volumes could not be obtained. Part of the plant capacity data dates from 2020-21.
  • Other news shares the windows: Ahluwalia’s results on the ban’s day 0, the Adani indictment and the Jan–Feb 2025 small-cap sell-off. The price evidence is weak throughout.
